The project

From satellite scenes to useful coastal evidence.

The project aims to automate a repeatable workflow for observing shoreline position and summarizing possible erosion, accretion, or relative stability across three Alexandria coastal sectors.

01

Collect imagery

Find open Sentinel-2 scenes for the Alexandria study area, apply a cloud-cover limit, and record download outcomes.

02

Extract shorelines

Calculate NDWI from Green Band 3 and Near-Infrared Band 8, create a water/land mask, and derive shoreline vectors.

03

Compare change

Compare available shoreline observations, estimate movement and annualized rates, and attach confidence information.

04

Store spatial data

Keep shoreline geometry, dates, sectors, metrics, and confidence scores in a PostgreSQL/PostGIS warehouse.

05

Serve insights

Use a Flask API to return risk, erosion rate, and confidence for supported coastal locations.

06

Explore the map

Present a year selector, shoreline overlays, risk markers, KPI cards, and a GeoJSON export in a Streamlit/Folium dashboard.

What the literature says

Methods are useful when their limits are visible.

The literature review supports satellite-based monitoring while emphasizing that extraction accuracy changes with the site, water level, imagery, and chosen method.

Water and land separation

McFeeters introduced NDWI to enhance open water in satellite imagery. The project applies the index to Sentinel-2 bands as a practical basis for shoreline extraction.

Egyptian coastal context

Previous work on the Nile Delta used multi-date Landsat imagery and DSAS to measure erosion, accretion, and stable coastline segments. Other Egyptian Mediterranean research shows how remote sensing helps track coastal change.

Accuracy depends on place

Sentinel-2 shoreline studies report varying performance across beaches and processing methods. Resolution, georeferencing, tides, water levels, and coast type can affect measured positions.

Interpretation note
The project uses one selected scene per year. Results describe shoreline positions on those image dates; they do not capture every seasonal fluctuation or short-term coastal event. Small measured changes should be read alongside the method's uncertainty.
Study design

A defined first version, with room to grow.

These boundaries keep the first deliverable focused and reproducible.

Included

  • Three named Alexandria coastal sectors
  • Sentinel-2 scenes from 2018 through 2026, subject to availability and quality
  • Python geospatial processing and NDWI shoreline extraction
  • PostGIS storage, Flask API, and Streamlit/Folium map
  • Risk labels, confidence information, and GeoJSON export

Planned as future work

  • Whole-coast coverage and other satellite sources
  • Seasonal or monthly shoreline time series
  • Automatic scheduled processing
  • Dual-map comparisons and expanded API features
  • Cloud deployment
